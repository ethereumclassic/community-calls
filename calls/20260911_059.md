### Where Should core-geth Live?

Diego put his position on the record, and lal probed the long-term plan.

- **Details**
  - Diego: There have been attempts, including a pull request on the website, to move core-geth from the etclabscore organisation into the ethereumclassic organisation. He opposes changing the recommended download repository
  - Diego: Moving it would send the wrong signal, because a headline of ETC is that nothing is official and the project is a joint effort of many parties. core-geth is one implementation of a specification. The specification and its ECIP process, which sometimes runs slow but works, is what belongs in the ethereumclassic repository as the social agreement or hard consensus
  - Diego: Every other implementation is opinionated by its developer and should live outside anything signalling an official organisation
  - lal: Asked what the long-term view is, since the current state is not ideal, and whether a community repo would attract more maintainers
  - Diego: The repository is fine where it is. Participation does not change where it should land. Becoming a maintainer works as in any open source project: show up, be open, demonstrate knowledge and real interest, and gain reputation. Telling real people from AI now makes vetting contributors slower
  - Diego: Maintaining core-geth is challenging, which is why he has pushed for new clients. The Nethermind plugin adds ETC support in around 2,000 lines, far easier to maintain than core-geth's tens of thousands
  - Istora: Keeping clients outside an official blessing adds decentralisation. If people disagree with a client's direction they can fork it, and if one stops being maintained they can switch. An official client forces decisions on behalf of node operators
  - Diego: Open source projects run on meritocracy and trust in maintainers rather than blockchain consensus. He is reachable by email, on social networks, and on Discord
- **Conclusion**
  - Diego's position is that the ECIP specification stays neutral in the ethereumclassic org, while clients, including core-geth, stay in their own organisations

### Paying Gas in Stablecoins

lal raised Ethereum's draft proposal, and the group weighed it.

- **Details**
  - lal: Asked about the draft proposal that would let Ethereum users pay gas in stablecoins
  - Diego: These are frame transactions, a more powerful successor to the idea behind EIP-7702. He hopes it reaches future hard forks, but his main fear is adoption. EIP-7702 was powerful but few wallets used it, so it never delivered the promised benefits
  - Diego: For ETC, most new transaction types from Ethereum rely on EIP-1559 concepts, so ETC would first need those concepts in the leanest form possible, such as Type 3 transactions, before adopting newer types
  - Istora: Asked how this differs from gas station networks and meta-transactions. Diego said those are conventions, whereas this would be part of the protocol itself
  - Diego: Does not know the details of pricing. He speculated the transaction could route through a DEX to convert the token into gas, since the mechanism is open
  - Istora: It solves a real UX problem where someone receives stablecoins but cannot send them without any Ether, though a backwards compatible workaround is to send a little Ether with the stablecoin
  - lal: Sees it as more flexibility and choice for users, and expects wallets to adopt it if the economics are better. Diego agreed adoption depends on wallets, but software features are now cheap enough to add and let die if unused
  - Istora: This feeds the ongoing ETC debate on layering features through hard forks versus ossifying
- **Conclusion**
  - Is ETC ready for new transaction types? Diego's view is that EIP-1559 concepts via Type 3 transactions must come first
