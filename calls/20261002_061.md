- Guest **Edilson Osório**, founder of [**OriginalMy**](https://originalmy.com), told the project's origin story: a 2015 tool to replace notary stamps in Brazil, pushback and threats from notaries, and his move to Estonia, from where he still runs the Brazilian business.
- After The DAO fork, OriginalMy rebuilt its signature-collection tool for citizen-proposed bills on a Bitcoin sidechain, then moved to **Ethereum Classic** when CryptoKitties congestion made Ethereum unreliable. - OriginalMy anchors to three chains: **Bitcoin** for trust, **ETC** for scalability and smart contracts, and [**Decred**](https://decred.org) for cheap Merkle-tree batching. On ETC a single smart contract opens a signing session and closes it once all signatories have signed.
- Its browser plugin for preserving web evidence produces court-ready reports. Edilson said Brazilian judges began accepting them in 2019, creating blockchain jurisprudence, at a fraction of the roughly $400 per page a notary charged.
- Edilson presented [**Hääl**](https://github.com/OriginalMy/originalmy-haal), his secret-voting protocol: identity unlinked from the vote through encrypted notes, homomorphic tallying, multi-party decryption of the result, revoting, and zero-knowledge receipts. - **Brolal** described Romania's proof-of-authority election blockchain and asked about validators and digital identity. Edilson argued permissioned validators can be acceptable only if the ledger is publicly auditable, and that identity must be unlinked from the ballot.
- Istora raised building [**OpenTimestamps**](https://opentimestamps.org)-style Merkle anchoring on ETC, including pinning proof-of-stake block headers as a proof-of-work fallback. Edilson agreed it made sense and did not believe anything similar existed on ETC yet.

## Guest

This week we welcome Edilson Osório of [OriginalMy](https://originalmy.com), a Brazilian e-governance platform built to fight bureaucracy and fraud. OriginalMy uses Ethereum Classic to anchor proofs of identity, digital signatures, and document authenticity, with the aim of making them strong enough to hold up in court.

The project began in 2016 collecting signatures for citizen-proposed bills in Brazil, and moved to Ethereum Classic for its immutability. Its tools include PACWeb for preserving web evidence, Open Prescription for digital medical prescriptions, and Hääl, a protocol for secret voting on public blockchains. ### OriginalMy's Origin Story

Edilson explained how OriginalMy began and how it arrived on Ethereum Classic.

- **Details**
  - Edilson: Brazil has around 13,000 notaries, and every signature on a contract needed a stamp from the notary where that signer was registered
  - Edilson: He first heard about Bitcoin in 2011 and mined it to understand the infrastructure, his background being infrastructure and cybersecurity. He returned in 2013, drawn by the idea that something digital could not be duplicated or stolen
  - Edilson: In 2014 he saw the University of Nicosia registering graduation certificates on Bitcoin. In early 2015 he built the first version of OriginalMy, focused on copyright because he had been a musician, and launched it mid-year
  - Edilson: A well-known Brazilian digital-law lawyer wrote that OriginalMy was "the Uber of the notaries". Notaries then followed him to his talks and he began receiving threats. In 2017 a deputy tried to pass a bill to criminalize cryptocurrencies, and his lawyers advised him to leave the country. He now lives in Estonia and manages the Brazilian business from there
  - Edilson: The same lawyer invited him to build a tool for collecting citizens' signatures on public bills. It won a Google social impact award in 2016 and some Brazilian states adopted the model, replacing signatures collected by hand
  - Edilson: He was building it on Ethereum when The DAO fork happened. Supporters asked how anyone could trust signatures presented to Congress on a chain that had been changed, so he rebuilt the tool on a full Bitcoin sidechain, batching signature authentications to Bitcoin
  - Edilson: When CryptoKitties congested Ethereum and transactions disappeared from the mempool, he took it as a signal to stop relying on Ethereum, integrated Ethereum Classic, and moved everything, including the signature-collection platform, to ETC
- **Conclusion**
  - OriginalMy came to ETC because of immutability: a chain that could be rewritten could not back legally meaningful signatures

### Bitcoin, Decred and Ethereum Classic

Kevin asked about OriginalMy's multi-chain architecture.

- **Details**
  - Edilson: OriginalMy uses Bitcoin, Ethereum Classic, and Decred, and has not added others
  - Edilson: He was interested in Decred's on-chain governance work, since OriginalMy is about e-governance. Decred built a tool based on Peter Todd's OpenTimestamps
  - Edilson: He won about 30 DCR in a Decred Twitter contest with a skydiving photo, integrated Decred, and used the prize to give the community free document registrations. It runs on his own infrastructure and has worked well
  - Edilson: Bitcoin is used for trust, Ethereum Classic for scalability and smart contracts, and Decred for cheap scaling. Projects doing hundreds of thousands of registrations in days would be too expensive even on ETC, while Decred's Merkle-tree batching handles them in one registration
  - Edilson: In one case, a Chinese electric power company signing a multi-million dollar contract with a Brazilian company asked for strong KYC and for the contract to be registered on both ETC and Bitcoin
- **Conclusion**
  - Each chain plays a different role, which gives OriginalMy trust, scale, and redundancy

### How OriginalMy Works, From Notaries to Court

Istora asked what exactly OriginalMy stores on chain.

- **Details**
  - Edilson: Documents are hashed. On Bitcoin the hash goes into an OP_RETURN, on ETC into a smart contract, and on Decred into a Merkle tree whose root is registered every hour
  - Edilson: The first signature version ran entirely on Bitcoin, encoding identification data in funded wallets and combining all parties into a final transaction, which was difficult to manage
  - Edilson: On ETC, one smart contract handles all signatures. Sending a document hash and the signatories opens a session, each signer submits a separate transaction, and the contract closes the session once all signatories have signed
  - Edilson: A browser plugin (PACWeb) preserves web evidence of fake news, defamation, and disinformation, used heavily around Brazilian elections. One click scans the page and generates a legal report, authenticated on more than one blockchain to fix its date
  - Edilson: Judges in Brazil started accepting these reports in 2019, so there is now blockchain jurisprudence in Brazil. Previously a notary viewed the page and wrote a report by hand, at around $400 per page; the plugin is available at any hour and is much cheaper
- **Conclusion**
  - Hashing plus a shared ETC signing contract underpins OriginalMy's documents, signatures, and court evidence

### Secret Voting With Hääl

Istora asked about the voting work mentioned in Edilson's earlier interviews.

- **Details**
  - Edilson: After moving to Estonia he designed a protocol for secret voting on public blockchains, studying Zcash's encrypted notes and Aztec on Ethereum. A proof of concept is on his GitHub
  - Edilson: A voter opens a session with their identity, and encrypted notes then unlink that identity from the vote. Votes are recorded on a smart contract using homomorphic encryption, and the final result is decrypted only when multiple parties sign together
  - Edilson: Only a voter's last vote counts, so voters can change their vote at any time, which counters coercion and vote buying. A zero-knowledge receipt proves a person voted without revealing how, and voters can choose to open their own vote to check it was recorded and counted
  - Edilson: The design drew on Estonian, Brazilian, and other national models. The protocol is called Hääl, Estonian for "voice"
  - Istora: Found the [originalmy-haal repository](https://github.com/OriginalMy/originalmy-haal) and said the work was ahead of its time, given how popular zero-knowledge proofs have since become. Edilson said that in 2018 and 2019 it was the very beginning, and the zero-knowledge proof was already verified on chain
  - Edilson: Universities have studied it, it became a master's thesis, and it is studied at MIT in MBA programs
  - Edilson: In 2020 OriginalMy ran a booth at a polling zone during Brazil's municipal elections, where voters tried a digital version after voting officially. Officials told him it was better than what IBM and Microsoft had presented, citing the multi-party decryption of results
  - Edilson: It was not adopted for political reasons. The president at the time was pushing to add printers to voting machines, which Edilson called a regression, while his model would have let people vote securely from anywhere
- **Conclusion**
  - Hääl showed secret ballots on a public chain were technically workable, but government adoption has not followed

### Romania's Blockchain Elections

Brolal brought news on blockchain in Romania's elections and put three questions to Edilson.

- **Details**
  - Brolal: Romania has used blockchain in its election infrastructure since 2020, operated by the Special Telecommunications Service, with a permissioned proof-of-authority model where trusted institutions act as validators and election data fingerprints are anchored in the European Blockchain Services Infrastructure. Romania has also introduced biometric electronic ID cards, which he believed were built with Thales
  - Brolal: Do several state institutions as validators decentralize an election chain, or just distribute control among authorities?
  - Edilson: Governments cannot be trusted. Brazil's proof-of-authority CBDC pilot ran with banks, and if Brazil's six big banks "have a barbecue" they decide everything on that network. If citizens cannot access and audit the ledger themselves, being a blockchain means nothing. If Vitalik had to persuade miners to change Ethereum after The DAO, a private chain would be easier still to change
  - Istora: Agreed that having a blockchain does not guarantee it is decentralized enough, and noted that ETC continued to exist even though a majority of miners forked, which shows the resilience of public chains
  - Brolal: Who should operate validators: only state institutions, or also universities, NGOs, and observers?
  - Edilson: Universities and observers are important. He has no problem with proof of authority for security, especially as AI accelerates hacks, but the ledger must be public so anyone can keep a copy, count open sessions and votes, and draw conclusions without seeing how anyone voted. A fully open chain such as ETC would be better, though he doubts governments will give up permissioning
  - Brolal: Should biometric identity and election systems ever interact?
  - Edilson: Any identity method can be used, including government ID, as long as identity is unlinked from the voting session. Votes can also pass through a mixer before reaching the chain to prevent enumeration by timing
  - Brolal asked whether combined identity and voting data could enable election prediction. Edilson: Not if identity is unlinked and votes are encrypted, since homomorphic tallying reveals only the final result, so nobody can see who is winning before voting ends
- **Conclusion**
  - Can a permissioned election chain be trustworthy if its ledger is public and identity is kept apart from the ballot?

### What Customers See, and OpenTimestamps

Kevin asked how OriginalMy's customers relate to the blockchain underneath.

- **Details**
  - Kevin: OriginalMy is a blockchain product with real-world use, not a token tacked onto something that could run on AWS. Do customers even know it uses blockchain?
  - Edilson: Early on OriginalMy used the word blockchain to attract investors, but never raised money. Brazilian VCs did not understand it, and Estonian investors were not interested in a Brazil-focused market
  - Edilson: After scams and ICOs, OriginalMy removed the term everywhere and now explains the solution: easy, reliable, and verifiable without trusting the company. If OriginalMy disappeared tomorrow, customers could still validate everything
  - Edilson: OriginalMy was the first blockchain company in Brazil. He has taught federal, state, and municipal governments, regulators, the central bank, and universities, still teaches with his wife, and this week gave a class at a university in Ghana
  - Kevin: What would customers lose if blockchains disappeared? Edilson: Bitcoin, ETC, and Decred would all have to disappear together, and in that case blockchain would be people's smallest problem
  - Istora: Asked about an OpenTimestamps-like Merkle-tree tool on ETC, and whether, as a proof-of-work chain, ETC could pin proof-of-stake block headers as a fallback, especially given the quantum threat to their consensus layers
  - Edilson: It makes sense. Merkle batching keeps costs manageable if ETC's price and fees rise, and he believed nothing like it existed on ETC yet
  - Edilson, on future ETC integrations: His interest is identity and zero-knowledge proofs. Verifying zero-knowledge proofs (he mentioned STARKs) and bringing secure digital identity to ETC would help OriginalMy and other projects
  - Edilson: He has been away from the community for a couple of years, since the fight over Charles Hoskinson's treasury proposal, when his work with OriginalMy was consuming his time
  - Istora: A current proposal would bring newer cryptographic primitives from Ethereum mainnet to ETC, which should enable use cases such as zero-knowledge proofs and voting protocols
- **Conclusion**
  - Should ETC have its own OpenTimestamps-style anchoring service, and could it serve as a proof-of-work fallback for other chains?

### Like Wolverine: Why OriginalMy Stayed on ETC

Kevin noted that many projects left after the 2019 and 2020 reorg attacks, while OriginalMy stayed for nearly seven years.

- **Details**
  - Edilson: The ETC community is strong and believes what he believes: code is law and proof of work works. ETC is "like Wolverine": attacked by every weapon, but resilient. OriginalMy's contribution is small, but it shows miners that someone believes alongside them
  - Brolal: Invited Edilson to write an article for ethereumclassic.org. Edilson said he was honored and asked to be sent topics
  - Edilson, on why the attacks did not scare him off: OriginalMy runs its own ETC node and keeps copies, so the past was preserved. He watched that at least one miner was still working and that the community was responding, and could have added another chain for future registrations if needed
  - Edilson: Only a small number of authentications, around a hundred, were affected. After Bitcoin's transaction malleability attacks he had built his system to track the content being registered rather than transaction IDs, and it automatically re-sent transactions orphaned by the reorg
  - Edilson: The attacks did not make his system stronger, but they made Ethereum Classic stronger. Brolal agreed that a system under attack becomes more resilient
  - Edilson, on future plans: He intends to stay on ETC for as long as he can. During the attacks he took part in the discussions, raised Decred as an example when anchoring to other chains was proposed, and recalled RSK's Sergio Lerner and Charles joining. The worst thing for a chain is a community that does not respond
- **Conclusion**
  - Edilson attributed his long stay to self-reliance (his own node and copies) and to a community that kept responding

### Advice to Builders and Wrap-up

Kevin asked what Edilson would tell developers who think ETC is dead.

- **Details**
  - Edilson: There is no emperor setting the rules. ETC is decentralized enough, cheap to test on, EVM compatible with smart contracts, and secure enough to build on. People who believe in proof of work should come to ETC
  - Edilson: Cited a reported incident from the previous day involving MetaMask and staking rewards as an example of proof-of-stake risk. In proof of work you must work to prove something; in proof of stake you only need money
  - Edilson: Most people in crypto are there for price, but value matters more and price is a side effect. His daily show covers how technologies work, with no price talk, recommendations, or partnerships with tokens or exchanges
  - Kevin asked him to finish the sentence "We anchor to Ethereum Classic because". Edilson: It is reliable enough never to undo a transaction, as happened on Ethereum with The DAO, because its people believe code is law and blockchains should be immutable
  - Edilson: He streams every morning (8 a.m. in Brazil, 2 p.m. in Estonia) as eddieoz on Kick, Twitch, and YouTube, and uses the same handle on Twitter and Bluesky. Istora said the links would go in the agenda notes and YouTube description
  - Istora asked whether he plans to attend Devcon in Mumbai. Edilson: He plans to, but needs a sponsor to fund the trip
