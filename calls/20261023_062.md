### TOKEN2049 and Bunker Mode

Istora turned to [TOKEN2049](https://classix.dev/reflections-on-token2049) in Singapore, where Zcash drew many of the talks, and to warnings published during the conference.

- **Details**
  - Istora: Zcash was a star project at TOKEN2049, and as a proof-of-work chain it offers inspiration for ETC. During the conference, [Justin Drake](https://x.com/drakefjustin/status/2107837081313505768) and [Vitalik Buterin](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2107976296320106851) warned that AI-driven advances in mathematics could undermine the elliptic-curve cryptography (ECDSA) behind most blockchains' signatures
  - Diego: They did not name a specific threat, but said serious attacks on elliptic curves may come before Q-Day, when quantum computers become available. They suggested rehearsing ["bunker mode"](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/08/bitcoin-and-ether-holders-urged-to-prepare-bunker-mode-against-possible-ai-attacks)
  - Diego: Addresses are hashes of public keys, and hashes are considered quantum-safe, but every transaction reveals the public key. Bunker mode means moving funds to a fresh address that has never been used on-chain
  - Istora: People should not panic. A rushed migration is more likely to cause losses than the attack. You cannot test-spend from the new address without exposing it, so check that the key matches the address another way, for example by trusting a hardware wallet. Smaller holders are unlikely to be early targets
  - Istora: Using the new BigQuery dataset, he published [how much ETC sits behind exposed public keys](https://classix.dev/etc-key-exposure). A large share of ETC is exposed, mostly in small balances. The DAO hacker's address, with 3.36 million ETC on a key unused for years, is by far the most exposed. Active large holders could migrate quickly; dormant ones need to be made aware
  - Kevin asked whether funds in a fresh address must never move again. Diego: Not never, but a future quantum-safe scheme will most likely need funds moved to a new address rather than reuse the old one, ideally in one controlled transaction, perhaps via a private miner
  - Diego: Recent AI work on cryptography also brought some good news: he said the origin of the seed behind ETC's secp256k1 curve has been explained, easing old fears of a backdoor. Istora: The good news is that cryptography is not backdoored, the bad news is that it may all be broken soon
  - Istora: ETC is better placed than proof-of-stake chains. Its hash-based proof-of-work consensus layer does not need to migrate, only execution-layer transactions. Diego: Even with broken elliptic curves, no one can forge blocks on a proof-of-work chain, unlike on Ethereum
- **Conclusion**
  - Holders should plan a careful move to fresh addresses rather than panic, and ETC's proof of work limits the problem to the execution layer

### Why Hash-Based Signatures

Istora tied the threat to the choice of post-quantum signatures for ETC.

- **Details**
  - Istora: The Zcash talks grouped signatures into lattice-based, hash-based, and classic ECDSA. Hash-based signatures look the safest, for the same reason proof of work is secure
  - Istora: If AI-assisted mathematics weakens lattice-based schemes, the [ML-DSA precompile proposal](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/ECIPs/pull/557) may not be the best approach, and the threats point towards hash-based signatures for ETC
  - Kevin: So ETC is safer because it has no validators to migrate. Istora: Yes. Hash-based signatures need much more data, which would cut throughput or validator counts on post-quantum proof-of-stake chains
  - Diego: [Ethereum's post-quantum researchers](https://ethereum.org/roadmap/security/quantum-resistance/) are exploring signature aggregation and zero-knowledge proofs of key ownership, so accounts are not tied to a single algorithm. It is unclear which algorithms will hold: he said a scheme called Hawk, considered as safe as Falcon or ML-DSA a year ago, had recently been broken
  - Istora: The field is still evolving, but confining the problem to the execution layer makes it easier for ETC to deal with
- **Conclusion**
  - Hash-based signatures look like the most likely long-term path for ETC, with the precompile question still open

### Preconfirmations, Stablecoins and Bridges

As the hour came up, Kevin asked about other ideas from TOKEN2049.

- **Details**
  - Istora, on preconfirmations: Proof-of-work block times vary, which makes in-person payments slow. [Zcash research](https://forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/private-preconfs-above-and-beyond-25s/57653) proposes that mining pools give a signed receipt guaranteeing future inclusion, which a merchant can accept as near-instant. It is a payments UX improvement and does not change exchange confirmation counts, which depend on reorg risk and should fall as hashrate grows
  - Istora, on stablecoins: Native stablecoins are preferable to bridged ones, given past problems like the [Multichain collapse](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/multichain-halts-use-of-crypto-bridge-saying-ceo-arrested/). A native issuer on ETC would give the first trading pair for DeFi and could start a flywheel
  - Istora: [Custody and infrastructure providers](https://ethereumclassic.org/blog/2025-12-14-institutional-crypto-custody-providers/) matter too. [Fireblocks](https://www.fireblocks.com) does not support ETC, and vendors at TOKEN2049 said they would support ETC if Fireblocks did. A dialogue has started. Asked what it would take, Istora said money: ETC has no foundation or treasury by design, so support would need an existing stakeholder to fund it, or natural growth
  - Kevin: Coinbase Prime and BitGo already offer custody. A stablecoin that could move to Ethereum or Base would grow ETC's DeFi ecosystem
  - Istora: Trustless bridges using zero-knowledge proofs of other chains' state, such as a rainbow bridge, could make ETC a proof-of-work anchor for assets. Kevin noted that the old PeaceBridge and ChainBridge relied on validators that are no longer active. Istora: ETC values self-reliance, so trustless designs are the ideal
  - Teasers for next call: Kevin mentioned ETCHD, and Istora said something related to the OpenTimestamps-style anchoring discussed with Edilson of OriginalMy may be announced within a week or so
