## Agenda

This episode covers ETC news since Call 60, client updates, takeaways from TOKEN2049, ETC in BigQuery, quantum and the ML-DSA precompile, the praxeology of Ethereum Classic, the rogue core-geth release, and Mumbai.

### Clients

- [Fukuii v0.10.0](https://github.com/chippr-robotics/fukuii/releases) (20 October) is the first non-prerelease after the 0.9.x series. It requires JDK 25 plus a mandatory JVM flag, and its RPC now refuses transactions without replay protection by default. Has anyone run it, and what would it take to recommend it to node operators?
- etclabscore core-geth
  - [#702](https://github.com/etclabscore/core-geth/pull/702) moved the toolchain to Go 1.25 and upstream v1.17.5. When should a v1.12.24 follow [Argos v1.12.23](https://github.com/etclabscore/core-geth/releases/tag/v1.12.23)?
  - [#704 `miner_getWorkFor`](https://github.com/etclabscore/core-geth/pull/704) would let a node hand out work for any coinbase, so pools could pay miners directly without holding their rewards. Would pools use it?

### TOKEN2049

- Istora and Diego spoke with more than 40 companies at TOKEN2049 in Singapore. What came out of it?
- Preconfirmations, modelled on a Zcash proposal: a service reserves the sender's next transaction slot and signs a receipt for the merchant, and pools that opt in earn most of the fee. No protocol change needed. Is there interest from pools and merchants?
- Institutions asked for custody support and stablecoins issued natively on ETC rather than bridged. Who should be approached first?
- Ethereum's post-quantum roadmap leans on hash-based signatures (leanXMSS) and account abstraction. Should ETC follow it rather than run its own track?

### BigQuery

- ETC is back in BigQuery: every block, transaction, log, token transfer, trace and balance from genesis, kept current every 10 minutes, with the same schema as Google's Ethereum dataset. Who else should know about it: analysts, exchanges, researchers?

### Quantum

- [ECIPs #557](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/ECIPs/pull/557) proposes an ML-DSA precompile. With Ethereum heading towards hash-based signatures, and [AI-assisted maths](https://x.com/drakefjustin/status/2107837081313505768) a possible risk to [lattice schemes](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2107976296320106851) as well as ECDSA, should the precompile target hash-based signatures instead of ML-DSA, or alongside it?
- Using the BigQuery dataset, the Call 58 follow-up measured [how much ETC sits behind exposed public keys](https://classix.dev/etc-key-exposure).
  - 83% of supply (132M ETC) sits behind exposed public keys, including about 21% held by exchanges and custodians.
  - 6.1M ETC is exposed only through signatures made on Ethereum. The 2016 DAO attacker's 3.36M ETC and several genesis allocations sit on keys that have been quiet for years.
  - Should there be outreach to exchanges and large holders to move funds to fresh, never-signed addresses?

### Philosophy

- [The Praxeology of Ethereum Classic](https://classix.dev/the-praxeology-of-ethereum-classic) starts from "blockchains exist" and derives censorship resistance, protocol neutrality and ossification. It argues that irregular state changes, a protocol-level treasury, and changing principles by edict all contradict the premise. Let's discuss.
- Should ETC write down a bar for "technical-only" hard forks? How would Bastion and the ML-DSA precompile measure up against it?
- Which single points of failure matter most today: clients and release signers, pools, exchanges, RPC endpoints, or funding?
- In the near future, everything discussed on these calls could be built automatically by agents: ECIP drafts, client pull requests, tweets, website changes. Contribunator already drafts tweets for [@eth_classic](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/tweets-eth_classic). What does that mean for how the call runs, and where should humans stay in the loop?
