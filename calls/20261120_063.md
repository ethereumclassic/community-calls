### ETC Cooperative

- The ETC Cooperative [announced on 11 November](https://etccooperative.org/posts/2026-11-11-etc-cooperative-to-wind-down-operations-en) that it is winding down, with most of the dissolution done by the end of 2026.
  - It no longer accepts grant applications. Questions and outstanding matters go to the Cooperative by 6 December.
  - It is arranging for the infrastructure it supports, including network nodes and the [Blockscout](https://etc.blockscout.com) explorer, to keep running for a transition period. Who should run this infrastructure in the long term?
  - Diego's role at the Cooperative ended on 7 November, after more than five years as its core developer. Thank you, Diego.

### Besu Plugin Support

- Besu lead Gary Schulte opened [besu#11887](https://github.com/besu-eth/besu/pull/11887) on 7 November. It lets plugins change the protocol rules from a given block or timestamp, so a chain's rules can live outside Besu. It is Part 1 of a series of plugin changes for chains other than Ethereum mainnet.
  - It builds on Diego's branch and credits him "for the design and most of the implementation". Diego has reviewed the rebase and offered two fixes, one for fork ID handling.
  - It targets Besu's `plugin-api-refactor` branch, which the Besu team expects to merge into `main` soon.
- The last piece for full ETC support is a small change that lets the genesis config carry [ETC-specific transitions](https://github.com/besu-eth/besu/compare/main...diega:besu:feat/genesis-custom-config-long).
- When both land, the [Besu ETC plugin](https://github.com/besu-etc/besu-etc-plugin) can run on unmodified upstream Besu, and ETC no longer needs its own [Besu fork](https://github.com/besu-etc/besu), as is already the case for Nethermind. What would it take for node operators and pools to run Besu on ETC?

### Quantum

- At Devcon 8, Justin Drake moved Ethereum's post-quantum target forward to December 2028, with three hard forks after Glamsterdam. Ethereum plans leanXMSS for validators and [leanSPHINCS](https://github.com/leanEthereum/leanSphincs) for user accounts.
- [The first payment using a quantum-safe signature on ETC](https://classix.dev/skinnycat) was made on 16 November with [skinnyCAT](https://skinnycat.classix.dev), an educational demo.
  - The leanSPHINCS signature is checked by an ordinary contract with no owner, using only opcodes ETC has today. No fork was needed.
  - Each signature is 7,740 bytes and costs about 123k gas of calldata plus 86k gas to verify. Ethereum's answer is to aggregate signatures with [EIP-8288](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8288). On ETC, miners would merge the proofs. Is that practical?
  - Only funds held inside the demo are protected. Protecting the 132M ETC behind exposed keys needs an ECIP.
- A proposed ETC "Quantum Strawmap" would separate what ETC already has through proof of work from what it needs, sequenced as ECIPs. What should it include?
- A Safe with a leanSPHINCS owner contract works on ETC today. Would large holders use a post-quantum multisig?
- [ECIPs #557](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/ECIPs/pull/557) (ML-DSA precompile) has had no activity since July. With Ethereum choosing hash-based signatures, should it continue?
