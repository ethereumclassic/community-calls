### Quantum

- At Devcon 8, Justin Drake moved Ethereum's post-quantum target forward to December 2028, with three hard forks after Glamsterdam. Ethereum plans leanXMSS for validators and [leanSPHINCS](https://github.com/leanEthereum/leanSphincs) for user accounts.
- [The first payment using a quantum-safe signature on ETC](https://classix.dev/skinnycat) was made on 16 November with [skinnyCAT](https://skinnycat.classix.dev), an educational demo.
  - The leanSPHINCS signature is checked by an ordinary contract with no owner, using only opcodes ETC has today. No fork was needed.
  - Each signature is 7,740 bytes and costs about 123k gas of calldata plus 86k gas to verify. Ethereum's answer is to aggregate signatures with [EIP-8288](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8288). On ETC, miners would merge the proofs. Is that practical?
  - Only funds held inside the demo are protected. Protecting the 132M ETC behind exposed keys needs an ECIP.
- A proposed ETC "Quantum Strawmap" would separate what ETC already has through proof of work from what it needs, sequenced as ECIPs. What should it include?
- A Safe with a leanSPHINCS owner contract works on ETC today. Would large holders use a post-quantum multisig?
- [ECIPs #557](https://github.com/ethereumclassic/ECIPs/pull/557) (ML-DSA precompile) has had no activity since July. With Ethereum choosing hash-based signatures, should it continue?

### Devcon 8 and the Quantum Deadline

Istora explained how ETC can respond to the quantum threat, the main reason he went to Devcon, and Marek raised the question of dormant coins.

- **Details**
  - Istora: Ethereum has a fairly clear answer. ETC has fewer moving parts: its data and consensus layers are hash-based and already quantum safe, so only ECDSA transaction signing in the execution layer needs to migrate. Ethereum plans to use the hash-based leanSPHINCS signature scheme
  - Diego: The Ethereum Foundation's cryptographers are taking the lead and thinking deeply about the best scheme and how to migrate users. The changes will most likely target Hegota, the fork after Glamsterdam. ETC should take part in the conversation and adopt what is needed, to stay compatible and to protect the network, because the threat is real
  - Istora: Ethereum's target is now December 2028, about two years away, so ETC should not wait around
  - Istora: Classix's [key exposure analysis](https://classix.dev/etc-key-exposure) found that most ETC sits at addresses whose public key is exposed. Large holders should move funds to a fresh address and not transact from it ("bunker mode"). Small holders are low-value targets
  - Marek: Many of those coins may be abandoned. Kevin: Long-untouched addresses are likely lost, like Satoshi's coins. Istora: Many early Ethereum holders, including genesis allocations, have exposed keys through Ethereum transactions. The biggest is the DAO hacker's 3.36 million ETC, and several genesis holders have about 0.5 million ETC each
  - Istora: A "proof of seed phrase" would let anyone whose keys came from a BIP-39 mnemonic prove ownership with a zero-knowledge proof, without revealing the public key. If a quantum attack became real, ECDSA could be switched off and only migrations allowed
  - Marek: That amounts to freezing coins, which changed the original rules, and which Bitcoiners are debating too. Given ETC's history, it is a question for the community's values. ETC's miners sign nothing, unlike proof of stake, so it may be less urgent
  - Istora: There is no clear answer. Freezing coins goes against the whole mantra. Only about 9% of ETC is both dormant and exposed, so he thinks it would take a catastrophic failure to justify intervening. Holders should move their funds as soon as possible
- **Conclusion**
  - Large holders should consider moving funds to fresh addresses, and any rule for dormant coins needs community discussion

### EIP-1559, ECIP-1120 and the ECIP Impasse

Istora set out the forks ETC would need to be post-quantum by 2028, and answered Marek's question on EIP-1559.

- **Details**
  - Istora: ETC needs to start adopting EIPs that are building blocks for post-quantum, and [EIP-1559](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559) is a key one because the post-quantum transaction types depend on it. Olympia is unlikely to be ready within two years
  - Istora: He proposed presenting an ETC version of Ethereum's post-quantum [strawmap](https://strawmap.org): Bastion first, which is already specified and simple, then [ECIP-1120](https://ecips.ethereumclassic.org/ECIPs/ecip-1120) for EIP-1559, then a further plan in about a year based on what Ethereum adopts, aiming for December 2028. This needs coordination from the ECIP editors
  - Marek asked whether EIP-1559's burn conflicts with ETC's fixed supply. Istora: Olympia would send the base fee to a DAO. ECIP-1120, proposed by him and Diego, returns it to miners over the next few blocks instead, which keeps the supply cap and keeps fees paying for security. It cannot go to the same block's miner because of myopic mining. 1120 needs updating with the contract design, and he hopes to propose it as part of an ETC post-quantum pathway before the end of the year
  - Diego: EIP-1559 adds the base fee, which every later transaction type reuses, so ETC must decide how to handle it before adopting any of them
  - Istora: There are four ECIP editors, and two of them want Olympia and are not talking to the other two. Given the deadline, he asked them to reconsider and agree on what everyone can support: a next hard fork with a subset of uncontroversial EIPs. If they will not engage, Bastion may have to move to Final call and be proposed as the next hard fork in early 2027. He offered this as an olive branch
- **Conclusion**
  - Istora will propose an ETC post-quantum pathway including Bastion and ECIP-1120, and has invited the other ECIP editors to work together

### skinnyCAT and Signature Aggregation

Istora described what ETC would need after those forks, and his skinnyCAT demo.

- **Details**
  - Istora: [skinnyCAT](https://classix.dev/skinnycat) is a small educational demo of the leanSPHINCS signature scheme. Last week he used it to make the first quantum-safe payment on ETC. It uses meta-transactions rather than a protocol change, costing about 300,000 gas, so it will not scale. ETC is technically already post-quantum, but not in a usable way
  - Istora: [EIP-8288](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8288), now being discussed on Ethereum, aggregates post-quantum signatures into a recursive SNARK, so the chain stores a small proof instead of large signatures. Frame transactions, not yet on Ethereum mainnet, would let non-ECDSA accounts pay for transactions
  - Diego: Agreed this is the right path. Ethereum already has pieces ETC lacks, which any solution will rely on
  - Kevin: Quantum and network safety should come first, then feature parity and the EEZ. Istora: They need not happen in series, but quantum security is the most important. ETC can survive without the EEZ, but probably not without quantum security
  - Istora: ETC should implement the same interfaces as Ethereum, so wallets and hardware wallets like Ledger and Trezor support it without custom logic. If wallets ask users "migrate to quantum, yes or no?", ETC users should be able to just say yes
  - Kevin asked about Kohaku. Istora: [Kohaku](https://notes.ethereum.org/@niard/KohakuRoadmap) is something different, an SDK for privacy on Ethereum that returns to cypherpunk ideas
- **Conclusion**
  - ETC should follow Ethereum's post-quantum path and interfaces closely

### Anonymity Pools for Mining Rewards

Istora asked Diego to explain his idea for mining pools and privacy.

- **Details**
  - Diego: Mining rewards could feed a privacy pool. Miners would redeem rewards with a zero-knowledge proof that they mined the block, and anyone could deposit into the same pool. Few people have an incentive to use anonymity pools, which keeps them small; here the protocol supplies a constant stream of deposits, so the anonymity set is large. It would need a protocol change and is low in the backlog
  - Kevin asked whether users would buy tokens from the pool. Diego: Not exactly. Users deposit and withdraw their own coins, which breaks the chain of transactions, and the larger the pool, the harder it is to track
  - Istora: Could it work without a protocol change, with each miner setting its coinbase to its own contract and regularly "reaping" rewards into the pool? Non-miners could pay miners a fee for the service. Diego: A coinbase reward is only a balance change with no execution, so it needs something triggering it, but it is possible. Istora: [EIP-7702](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702) and frame transactions could make it easier
  - Istora: Tornado Cash already runs on ETC and could be added to the Kohaku SDK, which is glue for using such protocols. Having ETC in that stack would be good when it is ready
- **Conclusion**
  - A privacy pool fed by mining rewards is worth exploring, but behind more urgent work
